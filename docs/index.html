Cuebright · Built end to end with AI · 2026

A Mac app that answers customer questions while you’re on the call

Cuebright transcribes your calls, answers questions from your own documents while you talk, and writes the summary and action items when you hang up. All of it runs on the Mac.

What I built

  • The Mac app: live transcription, answers from your documents, summaries and action items, accounts and people, and a Sales pack with deal notes and follow-up emails (coming in the next release).
  • The website, cuebright.com: product story, download, and download counting that respects privacy.
  • Everything around it: signed installers, automatic updates, and a test suite of 232 questions that every change has to pass.

I set the outcomes and made the product calls; AI coding agents wrote the code. From first prototype to 15 public releases in two months.

My approach

Viable, valuable, delivered

Shaped by Marty Cagan’s work, and tested in my own. Every product I work on has to pass three tests.

  • Viable

    Understand the business

    It has to work for the business: the market, the model and the numbers.

    For exampleGrew Brightcove’s OTT business from $7M to $10M ARR, and helped shape the analytics strategy behind the Wicket Labs acquisition.

  • Valuable & usable

    Understand customers and users

    People have to want to use it, and someone has to want to buy it.

    For exampleBuilt a 40+ customer design-partner program at Brightcove that increased engagement by 200–300%.

  • Delivered

    Get things done

    Ideas only count once they’re in customers’ hands.

    For exampleDelivered Broadcom’s AI workflow pilot 20% ahead of schedule, and took Cuebright from prototype to 15 releases in two months.

How I approach a new problem

  1. Listen first. Customers and users for their problems; internal stakeholders for the business and its constraints.30+ user interviews and weekly pilot feedback at Broadcom.
  2. Turn it into hypotheses. What to build, for whom, and what has to be true for it to work.At Broadcom, vague operational needs became hypotheses, success metrics and guardrails before anything was built.
  3. Reduce the biggest risk first. If we don’t know whether customers want it, test that. If we don’t know whether we can build it, engineering tackles that first.Early validation at PTC avoided about six months of unnecessary development.
  4. Work in small batches. Small MVPs and quick experiments, before a team commits months.Every Cuebright change was measured on 232 test questions; the ones that didn’t help were dropped.

What AI changes

Give teams context and outcomes, not tasks

As AI changes how software gets built, the most useful thing a product manager gives a team is context: the user, the customer and the business. Then ask for outcomes, so the team can make more decisions on its own and move faster.

That’s how I build Cuebright. I set the outcome and the context, AI coding agents do the building, and every result is measured before it ships.
